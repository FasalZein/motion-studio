# Scoring rubric

Score 1 to 10. Use the nearest evidence-backed anchor and explain intermediate scores. Score dimensions 1, 2 and 7 once per film and format. Score 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 for each shot. All applicable film and shot scores must reach 8. Use **unverified** instead of a number when necessary evidence cannot be read. A deliberately simple look or a hard cut can be correct for its brief. A hold is correct only as a **living hold**: something keeps moving (camera drift, an ambient layer, UI behavior, a growing callout) and the film stays inside the D59 liveness limits. A frozen hold earns no credit on any dimension. Consult the `motion-studio` genre playbook for timing bounds and evidence of message truth; do not replace these anchors with genre defaults.

| # / scale | 1 | 5 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Distinctiveness / film | Repeated default layout, type and entry across the film, with no relation to its stated look. | A named palette appears, but layout and motion mostly repeat familiar templates. | The chosen look is visible and adapted to the subject; layout and at least one signature move have a purpose. | The film has a coherent, recognizable visual language that grows from its message; variations remain intentional. |
| 2 Continuity and pacing / film | Unmotivated scene changes and frozen spans obscure the sequence: a short entrance, then a freeze, shot after shot (slideshow rhythm). | Some seams carry a thread, but repeated resets, frozen spans or rushed intervals interrupt reading. | Each seam carries its declared thread and motion runs through the cut; every hold is a living hold, and the liveness report passes the D59 limits or carries a G4 waiver. | Threads and varied rhythm make the sequence clearer; reading time comes from living holds, and no frame reads as a pause. |
| 3 Motion quality / shot | Movement jumps, stutters or uses one unsuitable speed or curve throughout. | Most motion is smooth, but starts, stops or timing feel uniform or at odds with the subject. | Movement has controlled acceleration and arrival; timing fits distance, emphasis and material. | Every move reads clearly at normal speed and on inspected transition frames; timing adds meaning. |
| 4 Restraint / shot | Several competing effects conceal the subject or claim. | Decorative motion or effects still compete with the main action. | Each effect or accent guides attention, connects states or expresses the intended style. | Removing any prominent effect would weaken understanding or intent; quiet moments stay alive with one calm, subordinate motion. |
| 5 Hierarchy and composition / shot | No clear focal subject, or essential information is unreadable. | A main subject is usually apparent, but contrast, spacing or simultaneous motion compete at key frames. | The subject and reading order are clear in key frames; spacing, contrast and protected elements work in the target format. | The shot remains legible through motion and framing changes; the composition guides attention precisely. |
| 6 Message truth / shot | Visuals contradict or fail to explain the stated message; visible factual claims lack sources. | The message is present in text, but visuals add little explanation or some claims lack traceable evidence. | Visual changes explain the stated message; every factual claim has a traceable source. Apply genre evidence from its playbook. | The visual sequence makes the message easy to retell after one view; claims and visual encoding remain accurate. |
| 7 Sound and sync / film | Measured hits and cuts are widely misaligned, or the mix is unusable. | The report shows noticeable offsets or inconsistent level, though key events can be followed. | The sync report shows **every intended SFX hit within 1 frame** of its event and **every beat-targeted cut within 1 frame** of a beat; measured loudness meets the mix target. | All events meet 8; the measured placement and level create intentional contrast without masking important sound. Confirm timbral judgment only if audio is heard. |
| 8 Technical finish / shot | Blank or clipped essential frames, unintended pops, or severe rendering defects. | Minor clipping, ghosting or color jumps remain at inspected frames. | Full-rate strips and sampled frames show no visible defect; blocking scan flags are resolved. | Frame-by-frame inspection, including fast motion and joins, finds no defect and repeated renders agree where tested. |

For dimension 2, read the packet's `liveness` and `seams` before the contact sheet; a 1 fps sheet cannot show whether a span is frozen or alive. `liveness.movingShare` is the content-basis moving share, the value the G4 gate checks (D72); `movingShareWholeFilm` is context only. Inspect each hold strip (`liveness.stillSpans[].file`) and each seam strip (`seams[].strip`) against its declared thread.

- A liveness report that fails without a current waiver caps dimension 2 at 7 (`liveness.dimension2Max` in `packet.json`). Quote the failed limits.
- A waived failure may score 8 only when the hold strips show the waived holds as deliberate; quote the waiver reason.
- No readable liveness report leaves dimension 2 **unverified**, not scored from the sheet.

For dimension 7, use the measured report, not a contact sheet or an imagined soundtrack. `motion-studio packet` derives it with this rule (`dimension7` in `packet.json`). The 8 anchor (every hit and beat-targeted cut within 1 frame) comes from the SPEC critique rubric, and the level limits (-14 LUFS within 0.5 LU, true peak at or below -1 dBTP) from D53. The other boundaries and the 5 and 2 scores are D69 (provisional): revisit them at the first eval run or tracer film.

- Timing error: each SFX hit's `|offsetFrames - plannedOffsetFrames|` and each beat-targeted cut's `|offsetFrames|`. A cut with an `offBeatCut` reason is judged against its declared timing and left out.
- Timing points for SFX hits: worst error at most 1 frame gives 8; more than 1 frame but under the window edge gives 5; at the window edge, or a hit not found, gives 2.
- Timing points for beat-targeted cuts: worst error at most 1 frame gives 8; 2 or 3 frames gives 5; 4 or more gives 2.
- Where the SFX window edge comes from: `mix` looks for each SFX peak only `syncWindowFrames` (3) frames either side of its planned peak, so an SFX error at that edge means the hit was not found within the window.
- Level points: within 0.5 LU of the target and true peak at or below -1 dBTP gives 8; within 2 LU and at or below 0 dBTP gives 5; otherwise 2.
- The score is the lowest of the timing and level points. Nothing timed leaves level only, with "no SFX hit tested". No report gives unverified.
- The rule stops at 8. Score 9 or 10 only when you heard the audio; otherwise mark `7-audible` unverified.
 Identify each intended beat-targeted cut from the board; an intentional off-beat cut is judged against its declared timing, not silently forced to a beat. When there are no SFX, assess only applicable checks and state that no SFX hit was tested. When music has no declared beat grid, mark beat alignment unverified. The project mix target is -14 integrated LUFS; quote its measured value and any documented tolerance rather than inventing one. The ledger can establish an SFX source, not how it sounds. If neither audio nor enough measurement is available, mark sound quality unverified even if numeric sync is measured.

The still comparison is separate from the eight scores. It is evidence for composition (dimension 5), not for motion: a render that matches its approved still proves nothing about easing, seams or holds, and a render that moves away from the still pose between key frames is not a departure. Compare each frozen approved still to the rendered frame at its exact global frame: **match** (approved visual intent remains), **drift** (noticeable but recoverable difference), or **departure** (the approval no longer describes the output). Give the reason and the frame.
